About

I am an Associate Professor in the Department of Computer Science at Purdue University and co-director of the Purdue Security Laboratory (PurSec Lab), affiliated with CERIAS. I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science and Engineering from Penn State University under the guidance of Patrick McDaniel, where I was the lead graduate student of the Systems and Internet Infrastructure Security Laboratory (SIIS).

My research investigates the security design and evaluation of software and systems on emerging computing platforms. Through systems design, program analysis, and formal methods, my work aims to improve the security and privacy of commodity systems. Focus areas include the Internet of Things (IoT), Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS), robotic vehicles, automobiles and self-driving cars, industrial control systems, and mobile systems such as smartphones, smartwatches, and AR/VR headsets.

Teaching

CS 426 Computer Security Undergraduate · Spring 2023 · syllabus

Principles and foundations of building secure computer systems, security best practices, and security failures in existing and emerging networks and systems. Four areas: cryptography and crypto protocols, network security, systems security, and privacy.

CS 590 IoT / CPS Security Graduate · Spring 2020, 2022 · syllabus

Latest research in the design and security of IoT and Cyber-Physical Systems: policy verification, safety/security foundations, problem detection and repair, voice-controlled devices, edge computing, industrial control systems, and autonomous vehicles.

CS 529 Security Analytics Graduate · Fall 2019–2022, 2024 · syllabus

Two parts: (1) Machine Learning for Security — algorithms, techniques, and applications in the security domain; (2) Security of Machine Learning Systems — attacks and defenses during learning and inference.
